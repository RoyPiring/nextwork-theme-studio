One loop, and it never asks for anything the site does not already know. Ticking a step is the only input. Every step is a bubble with XP on it; every bubble adds a piece to what the project is building; the finished thing goes into your world.

Two ways to build the same thing

The pieces are the same. The order is the difference, and it is the difference between two ways of working that every engineer will meet.

Waterfall
  1. Foundation
  2. Frame
  3. Walls
  4. Roof
  5. Windows
  6. Door
  7. Garden
Agile
  1. Tent
  2. Shed
  3. Cabin
  4. Cottage
  5. House
  6. + garden
  7. + garage

In waterfall nothing is usable until the last piece. In agile you can live in it after step one, and every step you can live in it a little better. A car goes skateboard → scooter → bicycle → motorbike → car. A data centre goes one server → a rack → a row → a room → a hall. Same XP either way; a very different feeling while you are in the middle of it — which is exactly what the two methods feel like on a real team.

Coins and gems

  • XP levels the role you chose. It only ever goes up.
  • Coins come with every step and buy cosmetics: a different roof, a pet, a fountain in the square.
  • Gems come one per finished project and unlock blueprints — the next tier of a building type. They are rare on purpose.

What the extension can actually read

Everything here runs inside the existing extension's rules: no network, no account, nothing leaves the machine. The world is built from three things the page already shows:

  • The project's tag and title — read from the page heading, the way the theme already reads it.
  • Which steps are ticked — the same checkboxes the page draws. That is what a bubble is.
  • Time on the page while the tab is in front — the focus timer already measures this.

State is stored where the themes are stored, so it survives reloads and never syncs anywhere.

Style

Isometric, soft-shaded, one light from the top-left with long shadows to the front. The newer Pokémon overworlds for the ground and trees, Clash of Clans for the way buildings sit on plots and drop in with a puff of dust. Every shape here is drawn in code from primitives — boxes, roofs, domes, wheels — so the demo needs no image files. A real build would want an artist's tileset, but the geometry is what it would be.

Open questions for the team

Does NextWork want completion read off the page, or would they rather hand the extension a small signal? Both work; the second is cleaner.
Does each project declare what it builds and how — house or car, agile or waterfall — or does the tag decide? Declaring it is a teaching opportunity.
Is the world per-account on their side one day, or does it stay in the browser? Staying is honest and free; moving it makes it portable.
